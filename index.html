Research

My research focuses on computational models that can understand, reconstruct and generate human motion from incomplete or noisy observations.

During my PhD I have investigated both sides of this problem: generative models that synthesise responsive human motion and reconstruction models that recover full-body motion from sparse consumer sensors. I am particularly interested in bringing these directions together to develop adaptive digital humans that can infer a user's state and generate contextually appropriate behaviour in real time.

My current work, Sparse2Motion, investigates full-body reconstruction from sparse VR headset and controller measurements using JEPA-based representation learning, adaptive computation and online test-time adaptation. I have also developed a paired VR–motion-capture dataset for studying the domain gap between synthetic sparse observations and measurements from real consumer hardware.
